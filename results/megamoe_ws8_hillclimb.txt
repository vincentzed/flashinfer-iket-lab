VERDICTS (world_size = 8)
1. Numerics-preserving (bitwise-exact) hillclimb: decode -1.8% (tile N 128->64 +
   epi_flag_batch (1,2)); prefill bf16 -0.8% (standalone token-back). Every accepted
   row verified torch.equal max|d|=0 against the baseline outputs.
2. Numerics-trading hillclimb (combine wire bf16 -> nvfp4, disclosed): -11.6% at 2048,
   -12.9% at 4096 - this clears the >=10% target. The wire change quantizes the
   per-topk fc2 terms to NVFP4 before cross-rank push-back (upstream's measured
   quality cost on GB200: ~1.8pt extra rel-L2 vs the bf16 wire; GSM8K-neutral in
   sglang PR #31470).
3. vs DeepGEMM megamoe (DeepSeek's own kernel, fp8-activation wire - NOT bit-comparable,
   both are real DSV4 serving configs): CuTeDSL wins prefill decisively on B300
   (-29.5% at 2048, -40.1% at 4096, vs deepgemm's numbers), including the 2048 case
   the sglang PR #31470 sweep had CuTeDSL losing before autotune. DeepGEMM keeps a
   4.5% decode edge over our best bit-exact config (0.3217 vs 0.3369); IKET shows
   decode is fc1 weight-TMA bound and the knob space is exhausted - closing the last
   gap needs kernel work (e.g. DeepGEMM-style ring-coupled fc2->combine).
Both kernels ran their GB200-derived heuristics on B300, plus the sweep shown here.

CORRECTION - THE 8.9ms MODE (paced re-bench, logs/megamoe_paced_bench.log +
IKET trace results/megamoe_m128_ikr_summary.txt):
The ~8.9-9.1 ms/launch readings are NOT steady kernel costs of ikr/mxfp8/sched4.
An IKET trace of the ikr config shows a clean 361 us instrumented launch with the
REDG combine costing only ~+2 us/task inside fc2_epi. With per-iteration
dist.barrier pacing (serving-like), all configs re-bench normally - and the 9 ms
mode instead captured the BASE config on that occasion. Verdict: the megamoe
kernel has a bistable degraded mode (~9 ms/launch, Dispatch_Barrier absorbing
~4.7 ms/launch in traces) triggered by launch pacing vs its cross-rank
stage/generation flow control, independent of config. Upstream-reportable, with
IKET trace evidence.
